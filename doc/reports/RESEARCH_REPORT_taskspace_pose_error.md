# Validating an RL Action-Space Comparison for Full-Pose Reaching, and Hardening Task-Space Controllers

## TL;DR

- **Your headline result is expected and defensible:** joint-space PPO actions beating differential-IK and operational-space actions for *free-space, full-pose reaching* is consistent with the strongest published action-space studies once you separate "free-space reaching" from "contact-rich manipulation" — the papers favoring task-space/impedance actions confine that advantage to contact-rich tasks, while the largest sim-to-real study (Aljalbout et al., RA-L 2024) found joint-space actions best overall on reaching and pushing.
- **One high-severity config bug fully explains IK-Abs's poor orientation tracking:** Isaac Lab does *not* normalize the policy's raw 4-D quaternion in absolute IK mode (confirmed from source), and a Gaussian policy emitting an unconstrained quaternion is a documented anti-pattern (Zhou et al. 2019; Schuck et al. 2026). A secondary asymmetry — joint actions are EMA-smoothed but task-space actions are not — further advantages joint control.
- **Top-3 fixes:** (1) replace IK-Abs's raw quaternion with a continuous 6-D or local tangent-vector rotation action and add EMA smoothing to task-space commands; (2) make the task-space policies *residual* on a scripted/model-based baseline; (3) raise/retune OSC translational stiffness and use per-dimension exploration std plus an orientation/distance curriculum. All are testable in single 36-minute runs.

---

## Key Findings

1. **The joint-space win is regime-appropriate, not anomalous.** Free-space reaching removes the entire rationale for task-space impedance (compliant force regulation) while keeping its costs (extra action dimensions, near-singularity misbehavior, discontinuous rotation parameterization). Expect the ranking to possibly invert in your later contact-rich cloth tasks.
2. **OSC seed-bimodality on the 6-DOF arms was a reference-feasibility failure, not a bug.** Commanding an infeasible gripper-down orientation drove the non-redundant arms into singularity-adjacent oscillation/divergence; removing the infeasible orientation (via FK-sampled reachable targets) eliminated all catastrophic divergence across 120 runs. This is exactly what IK/OSC theory predicts.
3. **The IK-Abs quaternion action is the single most impactful defect** and is a genuine, source-confirmed problem — not hypothetical.
4. **Your negative probes (longer episodes, larger IK deltas, wrist-in-Jacobian) are all theory-consistent** and should not be pursued further.
5. **The compliant-wrist A/B is clean evidence of capability extension, not redundancy assistance,** and its harm under torque-level OSC is a classic small-load-inertia oscillation problem.

## Details

### Q1 — Are the results sensible and expected? (Verdict: yes, largely; OSC diagnosis strongly confirmed)

**2.1 "Joint-space beats task-space for free-space full-pose reaching" — consistent once task type is pinned down.**
The most-cited opposing claim is Martín-Martín et al., *Variable Impedance Control in End-Effector Space (VICES)* (IROS 2019, arXiv:1906.08880), which advocates variable Cartesian impedance as an action space. But their evaluation is on "three prototypical manipulation tasks: Path Following (task with no contact), Door Opening (task with kinematic constraints), and Surface Wiping (task with continuous contact)" — and the VICES advantage shows up on the *contact/constrained* tasks, not on free-space path following. Varin, Grossman & Kuindersma (*A Comparison of Action Spaces for Learning Manipulation Tasks*, IROS 2019, arXiv:1908.08659) likewise find task-space impedance reduces samples on peg insertion, hammering and pushing — contact/dynamic tasks.

The decisive counterweight is Aljalbout, Frank, Karl & van der Smagt, *On the Role of the Action Space in Robot Manipulation Learning and Sim-to-Real Transfer* (arXiv:2312.03673, published IEEE RA-L 2024, vol. 9, pp. 5895–5902). They "train over 250 reinforcement learning (RL) agents in simulated reaching and pushing tasks, using 13 different control spaces" on a Franka Emika Panda, and conclude that "joint velocity-based action spaces show very favorable properties, and overall the best transfer performance," attributing action-space quality primarily to "the order of derivative and the emerging tracking error." A subsequent benchmark ("Benchmarking Action Spaces in RL for Vision-based Robotic Manipulation," arXiv:2606.18594) reproduces this: "the joint velocity action space is best for the vision-based picking and pushing tasks in terms of smoothness and final task performance."

**Reconciliation:** the literature does not contradict you. Task-space/impedance actions help *when the task is contact-rich or constrained*; your reach task is free-space (penetrable props, self-collision off, gravity off for the arm), which is precisely the regime where joint-space is expected to win. State this scope explicitly so readers do not over-generalize "joint-space is best" beyond free-space reaching. The reach task is a controller-hardening baseline; the real test of the task-space controllers is the later cloth manipulation, where the ranking may flip.

**2.2 OSC seed-bimodality chasing an infeasible orientation — a known failure mode.** Your fixed "gripper-down, free-yaw" box orientation was later shown geometrically unreachable over most of the box for the 6-DOF arms (~0% coverage for UR5e). Commanding a task-space controller to a pose outside the manipulator's image space is exactly Buss & Kim's described condition (*Selectively Damped Least Squares for Inverse Kinematics*, J. Graphics Tools 2005): "when the targets are too distant, the multibody's arms stretch out towards the targets, become close to a singularity and then oscillate or jitter." Modern IK analysis reinforces this: DLS/pseudo-inverse methods "may get stuck when a robot that is in a singularity is commanded to execute instantaneous EE motions that are not in the image space of the manipulator Jacobian" (analytically-informed IK, arXiv:2412.20409). For OSC an unreachable orientation reference becomes a persistent task-space error that the impedance law turns into a persistent wrench; near the wrist singularity the torque mapping blows up, producing the divergence you observed. The 7-DOF Kinova arms stayed tight (2.6–2.7 cm) because redundancy lets the nullspace absorb the infeasible orientation. Your remedy — remove the infeasible orientation, and Buss & Kim's target-distance clamping reduces oscillation — is the control-theoretic fix. That FK-sampled reachable targets eliminated catastrophic divergence in 120 runs is strong confirmation. **Diagnosis: correct and well-supported.**

**2.3 Probe outcomes — expected.** Longer 12 s episodes at a fixed 100k-step budget halve the number of distinct target poses seen, reducing goal diversity for on-policy PPO — consistent with 11.1 cm/27% vs 7.8 cm/56%. (This is the report's own reasoning grounded in standard on-policy sample-diversity arguments; I found no paper measuring exactly this reach ablation.) Larger per-step IK deltas (scale 1.0) push the DLS first-order linearization further from validity (Buss & Kim), increasing overshoot and worsening fine convergence (11.6 vs 8.3 cm) — expected.

**2.4 Compliant wrist: capability extension, harmful under torque-level OSC — plausible.** The matched-pair A/B is clean: on base-arm-reachable poses, wrist active/locked are within noise (4.7 vs 4.3 cm); on wrist-requiring poses only the active wrist succeeds (4.6 vs 10.3 cm bimodal). That signature is *added capability*, not redundancy assistance. The OSC harm is physically sensible: a near-zero-reflected-inertia, 3.5 N·m joint held only by PD 400/20 is exactly the "small load inertia vs actuator" regime Hurst et al. (*Series Elastic Actuation: Potential and Pitfalls*, 2004) flag as prone to high-frequency oscillation "the software controller cannot damp." Including it in the OSC effort path removes its damping and destabilizes the chain — matching your divergence report and the strictly-worse damped-compliant inclusion (15.1 cm/12% vs 9.0 cm/40%). Excluding it and letting its own PD hold it is correct.

### Q2 — Configuration audit

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **IK-Abs: raw 4-D Gaussian output used as quaternion, unnormalized** | **WRONG / anti-pattern** | Isaac Lab source (verified): `process_actions` does only `processed = raw * scale`; controller `set_command` sets `ee_quat_des = command[:,3:7]` with no normalization (normalization/shortest-path handling exists only in the *error* computation via `axis_angle_from_quat`, applied to the error quaternion). | 3 | **IK-Abs `initial_log_std = −1.0`; others 0.0** | **SUSPICIOUS (exploration coupling)** | Lower std concentrates exploration; combined with a discontinuous quaternion parameterization, the entropy bonus can still push toward large-magnitude jittery SO(3) actions (Schuck et al. 2026, "misguided entropy regularization"). A single global std across position+quaternion dims is suboptimal. |
| 4 | **Task-space actions NOT EMA-smoothed (joint IS, α=0.2)** | **SUSPICIOUS (unfair asymmetry)** | Raw per-step task-space commands induce jitter; joint gets a low-pass filter the others don't. Smoothness/derivative order is a primary driver of action-space quality (Aljalbout et al. 2024). |

Clamp 50–200 is inside defaults but soft; with critical damping and any residual/model error, translational stiffness ~100–200 leaves a static offset qualitatively consistent with your 8–14 cm. `d = 2·√(kp)·ratio` with ratio 1 is correct Isaac Lab critical-damping behavior. |
| 6 | **OSC partial inertial decoupling; no gravity comp (gravity off); nullspace only when redundant; arm PD zeroed** | **OK / defensible** | Isaac Lab OSC tutorial explicitly supports `partial_inertial_dynamics_decoupling`, `gravity_compensation=False`, `nullspace_control="position"` toward joint-limit centers. Gravity comp off is correct since gravity is disabled. |

### Q3 — Recommendations to close the task-space pose-error gap

Ranked by (expected impact × implementation cost). One 100k-step run ≈ 36 min on one GPU.

**Rank 1 — Fix IK-Abs's orientation representation (and audit IK-Rel), and EMA-smooth task-space actions. [High impact, low cost, directly testable].**
Stop feeding a raw 4-D Gaussian "quaternion." Either (a) output a **6-D continuous rotation** (two 3-vectors → Gram–Schmidt → matrix → quaternion for the controller), or (b) switch IK-Abs to **absolute position + local tangent-vector delta orientation** like IK-Rel's rotation channel. Add EMA (α≈0.2) on the task-space command stream. *Evidence:* Zhou et al. (2019) report that "the proposed 6D and 5D representations do not produce errors higher than 2°," whereas for quaternion, axis-angle and Euler "certain test samples still produce errors up to 180°"; their 6D reaches "around 95% of errors lower than 5°, while Euler … is the worst." Schuck et al. (2026, ICLR) show for RL specifically that local-frame tangent vectors are the most reliable across PPO/SAC/TD3, and that for fixed target poses global matrices/quaternions can match deltas *only if* properly projected/unit-centered — never as raw Gaussian outputs. The unnormalized-quaternion path is source-confirmed, so this is a real defect. *Expected effect:* largest single lever on **pose%** for IK-Abs, concentrated on worst-case reorientations (where your pose% is lost); EMA modestly reduces mean position error and variance. *Test:* UR5e-F140 and Kinova-F140 IK-Abs, 6-D rotation vs quaternion baseline, 5 seeds each (~6 h).

**Rank 2 — Residual / hybrid task-space control. [High impact, medium cost].**
Compute a scripted nominal task-space command each step (proportional step from current EE pose toward the target, clamped) and let the policy output a *residual* pose (and, for OSC, residual stiffness); feed nominal+residual to the DLS/OSC controller. *Evidence:* Johannink et al., *Residual RL for Robot Control* (ICRA 2019, arXiv:1812.03201) and Jin et al. (*From Imitation to Refinement*, arXiv:2407.16677) show residual schemes combine base-controller efficiency with RL adaptability, improving sample efficiency and final accuracy. *Expected effect:* should substantially cut mean position error for all three task-space controllers since the analytic baseline already solves most of the reach. *Test:* UR5e-F140 IK-Rel and OSC, residual vs plain, 5 seeds; benchmark against joint-control's 3–5 cm.

**Rank 3 — OSC-side tuning. [Medium impact, low-medium cost].**
(i) Widen the variable-kp clamp toward **300–500** on translational axes (keep rotational moderate), staying under Isaac Lab's (0,1000) ceiling; (ii) per-dimension policy std (smaller initial log_std on orientation and stiffness dims); (iii) add SVD singular-value clamping / selective damping to the DLS IK (Buss & Kim 2005) and/or a small λ increase near singularities; (iv) consider a manipulability-maximizing nullspace objective rather than default-config attraction on redundant arms. *Evidence:* higher Cartesian stiffness improves tracking (Nakanishi et al. 2008; distributed-OSC arXiv:1701.05929 shows a 4.7× stiffness increase improved tracking); SDLS reduces near-singular oscillation without ad-hoc damping constants (Buss & Kim 2005). *Expected effect:* incremental — a few cm off OSC mean error and modest pose% gains; unlikely alone to beat joint control. *Test:* OSC stiffness sweep (50–200 vs 100–400 vs 200–500) on UR10-F140, 3 seeds.

**Rank 4 — Curriculum over orientation difficulty / target distance. [Medium impact, medium cost].**
Start with targets near the default pose / small reorientations, expand to the full FK distribution as pose% rises. *Evidence:* Florensa et al. (*Reverse Curriculum Generation*, CoRL 2017, arXiv:1707.05300) and UR5e FK-reach curriculum work (arXiv:2002.02697) show curricula improve goal-reaching convergence; especially relevant since discontinuous-orientation pain is worst for large reorientations. *Test:* IK-Abs with vs without orientation curriculum, UR5e-F140.

**Rank 5 — Sampler refinement (manipulability weighting). [Low impact, low cost].**
Weight FK sampling by manipulability (or reject near-singular configs) for a more Cartesian-uniform, less singularity-dense target set. *Evidence:* uniform joint sampling over-represents singular configs (Reuleaux arXiv:1710.01328; arXiv:2001.08042). *Expected effect:* small; mainly trims the hardest OSC/IK targets and tail error.

**Fixes NOT recommended (evidence-negative in your own probes):** longer episodes; larger per-step IK delta scale; including the compliant wrist in the OSC Jacobian. All three were tested and were strictly worse, consistent with theory.

## Recommendations (staged, with decision thresholds)

- **Stage 0 (do now, cheapest, highest yield):** Implement Rank 1 — 6-D/tangent-vector orientation action for IK-Abs + EMA on task-space commands. **Threshold to continue:** if IK-Abs pose% on UR5e-F140 rises materially toward joint-control levels (target: closing at least half the pose% gap, i.e. from ~22% toward ~50%+), the representation was the dominant problem and you should roll the same change into IK-Rel and re-run the full 24-variant grid.
- **Stage 1:** If Stage 0 helps but a mean-position gap to joint control remains (>2× at ~7–8 cm vs 3–5 cm), add Rank 2 (residual control) to IK-Rel and OSC. **Threshold:** residual variants reaching ≤5 cm mean position error would make the task-space controllers viable to carry into the cloth tasks.
- **Stage 2:** In parallel with Stage 1, run the Rank 3 OSC stiffness/exploration sweep. **Threshold:** if no clamp setting brings OSC below ~7 cm / above ~30% pose, stop tuning OSC for the free-space baseline and rely on OSC only where contact makes impedance actively useful (the cloth tasks), where its ranking is expected to improve.
- **Stage 3 (only if convergence, not asymptote, is the bottleneck):** add the Rank 4 curriculum. **Threshold:** adopt if it improves worst-seed pose% or reduces seed variance without lowering the mean.
- **Reporting:** explicitly scope the joint-space-wins conclusion to free-space reaching, and preserve the box-vs-FK comparison as your evidence that the earlier OSC divergence was reference-feasibility-driven.

## Caveats

- **Framework specifics were verified against Isaac Lab source/docs** (OSC cfg defaults `motion_stiffness_limits_task=(0,1000)`, `d=2√(kp)·ratio`, `partial_inertial_dynamics_decoupling`, `nullspace_control`; DifferentialIK 7-D absolute action; confirmed absence of quaternion normalization in absolute IK, with shortest-path handling only inside the pose-*error* computation). Some of the deepest source lines were cross-checked via official GitHub issues/discussions and a third-party docs mirror; they were consistent, but treat the exact line-level quotes as high-confidence-not-certain.
- **The "longer episodes hurt PPO at fixed steps" explanation (§2.3) is the report's own reasoning** grounded in standard on-policy sample-diversity arguments; I did not find a peer-reviewed paper measuring that exact reach ablation.
- **The mapping from OSC stiffness magnitude to your specific 8–14 cm static offset is qualitative,** not a fitted model; use the Rank-3 sweep to establish the real curve.
- **The action-dimension disadvantage (#8) is a general PPO result;** it is a plausible contributor to (not a proven cause of) the OSC/IK-Abs gap in your setup, and it is confounded with the representation and smoothing issues, so isolate it by holding action-dim fixed where possible.
- **Literature is genuinely split by task regime,** not by outright disagreement: nearly all pro-task-space evidence is contact-rich. Your free-space finding and the contact-rich literature can both be true, which is exactly why the cloth-task re-evaluation matters.
