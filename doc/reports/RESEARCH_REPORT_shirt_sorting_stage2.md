# Design and Roadmap for a Camera-Realistic, Cooperative Shirt-Sorting Pipeline

## 1. Executive Summary

The single most important finding: **the presentation-quality ceiling is set by the grasp pair, and the first grasp is chosen in Task 1 — so the highest-leverage change is to make Task 1 produce hem-corner-region first grasps and to make Task 2 cooperative (a movable/active holder), not to keep optimizing robot 2 alone.** One-sided learning by robot 2 has stalled at present 0.380 @ coverage 0.637 for the winning hem↔hem pair, and the project's own 15,000-trial study shows the oracle ceiling from an *arbitrary* first grasp is only 0.713 vs 0.820 for a hem↔hem pair — a ~0.11 coverage gap that robot 2 cannot recover no matter how well it learns.

Recommended architecture:
- **Task 1 (`shirt_pick`)**: keep the highest-point pick as the physically-robust bootstrap (it is the literature-standard depth-trivial grasp and semantic keypoints are unreliable on crumpled cloth), but add a *learned grasp-point refinement head* trained against downstream presentation value, and generate the real Task-1→Task-2 terminal bank so Task 2 trains on realized holder states rather than a synthetic hanging bank.
- **Task 2 (`shirt_present`)**: reformulate as a **two-agent cooperative task with a shared presentation reward** — but implement it first as a *single joint policy over both arms* (the FlingBot/SpeedFolding precedent), reserving MAPPO/IPPO for a controlled ablation. The dominant fix is letting robot 1 reposition its holding pose (within its no-x-translation constraint) and curriculum-filtering the hanging bank so the second hem corner starts low/reachable.
- **Task 3 (`shirt_distribute`)**: essentially done; the remaining work is bin-2 reachability and layout randomization, plus consuming the real Task-2 terminal bank. No presentation-relevant redesign needed beyond preserving the inspected state until the drop.
- **Observations**: adopt a per-task *observation contract* where every term maps to a real perception source (depth highest-point, segmentation-mask coverage, a 12-keypoint detector with per-keypoint visibility flags), keep the RL loop camera-free with calibrated noise + an asymmetric privileged critic now, and defer tiled-rendering vision to a teacher–student distillation stage.
- **Gripper fidelity**: keep the deterministic attachment for *training*, add a *stochastic grasp-success/slip gate* calibrated to published misgrasp/multi-layer statistics for *evaluation*, and run a one-off contact-physics pinch validation study. This is precisely the pattern the literature endorses.

Target metrics: Task-2 present ≥ 0.65 coverage start / 0.75 curriculum end at grasp rate ≥ 0.9 on the hem↔hem pair; no regression on pick (1.000) or distribute (0.882); a demonstrated chained end-to-end success from crumpled belt to correct drum.

## 2. Q1–Q5 Answers

### Q1 — Adapting the three single-agent tasks to maximize end-to-end presentation quality

**Evidence review.** The project's own study is the strongest evidence and it is internally consistent with the external literature. Hem_corner↔hem_corner is the winning pair (0.820 median coverage, matching the realizable oracle bound of 0.82); the oracle region→region policy from an arbitrary first grasp reaches only 0.713; regrasping during presentation hurts (−0.031 to −0.051), so robot 1 must keep holding. Externally, the in-air unfolding paradigm — grasp a point, lift, grasp a second point, stretch — is the canonical ancestor here. Maitin-Shepard et al. (ICRA 2010, "Cloth Grasp Point Detection … Robotic Towel Folding") demonstrated reliable in-air corner detection that "enables for the first time a robot … to reliably and fully-autonomously fold previously unseen towels, demonstrating success on all 50 out of 50 single-towel trials as well as on a pile of 5 towels." Doumanoglou et al. (2014, ICRA / ECCV "Active Random Forests") framed unfolding as active perception with a hanging garment, iteratively grasping the lowest point until an unfolded state is detected, and used Hough forests to locate semantic keypoints. The crucial nuance the student already flagged: Doumanoglou's iterative-regrasp *unfolding* is a multi-cycle process distinct from the single in-procedure regrasp the study showed to be harmful.

On whether hem corners are perceivable on a crumpled shirt: the literature says **no, not reliably when crumpled** — this is exactly why the study's highest-point bootstrap is correct. But once the shirt is *hanging* (post-Task-1), keypoints become tractable: Lips et al. (2024, "Learning Keypoints for Robotic Cloth Manipulation using Synthetic Data") report 74% mAP and 9-pixel average keypoint distance after real fine-tuning (≈ 1–2 cm at their resolution); Cloth Funnels (Canberk et al., 2022) trained a DeepLabv3 keypoint detector reaching ~5/128-pixel average error on canonicalized configurations. This supports the study's own finding that a keypoint-restricted chooser reaches the oracle — **perception-friendly keypoints are sufficient grasp targets on the hanging shirt, but not on the crumpled pile.**

**Recommendation (ranked options for the first-grasp problem):**
- **(a) Keep highest-point + oracle second-grasp policy in Task 2 (ceiling ≈ 0.713).** Lowest effort, robust, but leaves ~0.11 coverage on the table vs the hem↔hem ideal. This is the *fallback*.
- **(b) Pick-then-rehang cycles until a hem region is held (+~0.11 toward 0.82).** This is the Doumanoglou iterative-unfolding pattern. Cost: multiple regrasp cycles, each with the 5–7% post-latch slip risk in Task 1, and each cycle changes the holder to a garment extremity. **Reject as the primary path** — the study's own regrasp-hurts finding and the slip risk make repeated rehangs fragile.
- **(c) A learned Task-1 grasp-point policy trained against downstream presentation reward (RECOMMENDED).** This is affordance/value-map learning on cloth: FlingBot, Cloth Funnels, and SpeedFolding all learn *where to grasp* from a value/affordance map keyed to a downstream objective (coverage / canonicalized-alignment). Implement as a grasp-point refinement head on Task 1 whose reward is the *realized Task-2 coverage* of the resulting hold (bootstrapped from the study's JSON first-grasp→best-second-grasp table so the signal is dense, not sparse).
- **(d) Two-stage Task 2 where robot 2 first canonicalizes.** This is the Cloth Funnels "cloth state funnel" idea. Powerful but heavy; defer to future work given the two-arm / holder-must-keep-holding constraints.

**MDP/implementation changes:**
- Task 1: add observation terms for the study's 12 region-landmark keypoints (in sim, read from privileged particle state but *only the 12 landmark particles*, to keep the contract camera-realistic); add a border-distance feature for the grasp target (the study shows +0.08 monotone quality for grasping near an opening). Add a terminal-state snapshot at the present-latch to build the Task-1→2 bank. Reward: add a small terminal bonus proportional to the *predicted* downstream coverage of the achieved hold region (lookup from the study's table).
- Task 2 fixes independent of the pair question: **bank-filter the hanging states so the target hem corner starts low/near** (the bank stores the anchor particle and drape; select hem-corner-anchored states), add reach-assist resets, and use a target-conditioned grasp curriculum that starts with an accessible corner and raises it. Raise the success gate 0.50 → 0.65. Adopt the study's tautness-pulse finding (overstretch to 1.10, relax to 1.05) as legitimate policy — it halves settle time at no coverage cost.
- Task 3: preserve the inspected/taut state until the drop decision (add a "hold-presented-until-commanded" gate); otherwise this is the bin-2 reachability (ceiling ≈ 0.83, scripted baseline 0.88) + layout-randomization backlog.

**Chaining order (answers Q1.4).** Generate terminal banks *forward and last*: (1) freeze Task 1, generate its present-latch terminal bank *including* the 5–7% post-latch slip episodes (do not filter them — they are the real distribution Task 2 must survive); (2) retrain/curriculum Task 2 against that bank; (3) generate Task-2 terminal bank; (4) retrain Task 3 against it. Chaining distribution shift is the project's #1 flagged risk, so each seam is a decision gate.

**Decision gate that reverses (c) → (a):** if the learned Task-1 grasp head does not lift realized Task-2 coverage above the 0.713 arbitrary-first-grasp oracle within a fixed budget (say 100k trainer steps × 3 seeds), revert to highest-point + oracle-target Task 2 and bank the 0.713.

### Q2 — Should Task 2 be reformulated as multi-agent, with robot 1 active?

**Evidence review.** The motivating evidence is strong: robot 2 alone reaches 0.927 only with the easy lowest-point grasp and 0.380 with hem↔hem, and the study proves the pair (hence robot 1's pose) is the dominant lever. The formulation space:

- **(a) MAPPO/IPPO (two agents = two arms).** MAPPO (Yu et al., NeurIPS 2022 Datasets & Benchmarks, "The Surprising Effectiveness of PPO in Cooperative Multi-Agent Games") is a strong, well-benchmarked baseline that matches or beats off-policy methods on SMAC/MPE/Hanabi/GRF with a centralized critic. skrl 2.1.0 exposes IPPO and MAPPO, and Isaac Lab ships them for `Isaac-Shadow-Hand-Over-Direct-v0`. **Critical integration constraint:** Isaac Lab's multi-agent support (`DirectMARLEnv`) lives in the *direct*-workflow API; the manager-based API used by all three tasks is single-agent. Bridging options: (i) rewrite Task 2 as a `DirectMARLEnv` (clean, but a substantial rewrite and forfeits manager-based reward/observation term reuse); (ii) keep the manager-based env and treat both arms as **one joint-action single agent**; (iii) wrapper-level splitting via `multi_agent_to_single_agent`. There is a documented, unresolved gotcha: with gymnasium ≥ 1.0.0 skrl's MARL path breaks on `DirectMARLEnv.state()` (GitHub Toni-SM/skrl Discussion #238) — users pin `gymnasium==0.29.1` or patch out `env.state()`. Treat skrl MARL maturity as unverified in this repo.
- **(b) One joint policy over both arms (RECOMMENDED first).** This is what bimanual cloth work actually does: FlingBot learns a single dual-arm pick parameterization; SpeedFolding's BiMama-Net predicts *pairs* of gripper poses from one network; FabricFlowNet predicts both picks from one flow-based policy. **When both arms are simulated centrally with no communication constraint and full observability, CTDE buys you little** — the reason MAPPO exists (decentralized execution under partial observability) does not bind here. A single policy emitting the concatenated action of both arms sidesteps the entire manager-based/DirectMARLEnv problem and the lazy-agent pathology.
- **(c) Hierarchical/sequenced: a grasp-selection chooser + a continuous stretch controller.** This matches the study's structure (a region→region table feeding a low-level stretch) and the SpeedFolding/Cloth Funnels "predict poses, then execute primitive" decomposition. Strong candidate for the *grasp-choice* layer specifically.
- **(d) Keep robot 1 scripted but movable (RECOMMENDED as the concrete cooperation).** The study says robot 1 must *keep holding* — its cooperation is holding-pose control, not release. A scripted-but-repositioning holder (moving to a study-informed pose, respecting the tensegrity's no-x-translation) captures most of the cooperative gain with the least ML risk.

**Recommendation with decision criteria.** Stage the cooperation: **(d) movable scripted holder → (b) single joint policy → (a) MAPPO only as an ablation.** Move from single-agent to the joint policy when, after the Q1 curricula, the hem↔hem grasp rate is learnable (e.g. > 0.6) but the *coverage ceiling* with a fixed holder pose stays below ~0.75 — i.e. the evidence shows the holder pose, not robot 2's skill, is the binding constraint. Escalate to MAPPO/IPPO only if a single joint policy exhibits a measurable coordination failure that a centralized critic would fix, which is unlikely under full observability.

**Feasibility constraints respected.** Robot 1 is the 5-DOF tensegrity with weak wrist torques and no x-translation — so its "action" in the joint policy should be a low-dimensional holding-pose adjustment (y, z, and the accessible wrist DOF), not a full manipulation policy. Two-arm stretching is validated only to tautness 1.15, so the pull-target shaping must cap there. The 64-env throughput knee bounds sample budgets — favor the sample-efficient single policy over MARL's larger effective sample needs. Grasps are attachment slots, so no handover physics is needed.

**Credit assignment / reward design.** Use a **shared presentation reward** (coverage + tautness-band + stillness) as the team objective, plus light per-arm shaping (reach shaping for robot 2's grasp, holding-pose-stability shaping for robot 1) to avoid sparse-reward stalls. Known MARL pathologies to watch: the **lazy-agent problem** (Sunehag et al. 2018, VDN — an agent goes complacent under a shared reward) and non-stationarity. Both are *muted* here because the setting is 2-agent, fully observable, and centrally simulated — which is precisely the regime where a single joint policy or a centralized critic is safe. Do **not** use purely local rewards (they kill cooperation) and do **not** penalize "gripper closed while far" (the project's hard-won lesson: grasp rate → 0).

**Decision gate that reverses "go cooperative":** if the movable-holder + single-joint-policy path does not beat the best single-agent Task-2 result (0.927 lowest-point / target 0.65+ hem↔hem) on unseen-seed deterministic eval, stop and ship the single-agent oracle-target policy.

### Q3 — Redesigning observations to be camera-obtainable; should camera sim be in the loop?

**Per-task observation contract (real perception source for each term):**
- *Shirt centroid / relative positions*: segmented point cloud (depth + foreground segmentation) down-sampled to N points, centroid computed — this is exactly UniFolding's and VCD's input. Noise: depth-camera plane distance; calibrate from the front/back stereo baseline.
- *Grasp-target (highest point)*: depth argmax after segmentation — the literature-standard depth-trivial grasp; essentially noise-free in sim, robust in reality.
- *Targeted hem corner / 12 region keypoints + per-keypoint visibility flags*: a keypoint detector (Lips et al. 2024: 74% mAP, 9-px ≈ 1–2 cm error after fine-tuning; Cloth Funnels DeepLabv3: ~5/128-px). Model the detector as: position noise ~1–2 cm Gaussian + per-keypoint dropout at (1 − recall) with visibility gated by the study's own occlusion metric.
- *Coverage*: segmentation-mask area ÷ calibrated flat area — directly measurable from the front+back masks (the study's own metric).
- *Tautness ratio*: **load-bearing sim2real gap** — not directly observable; must be *estimated* from the visible silhouette aspect ratio or dropped from the actor and kept only in the privileged critic.
- *Grasp-active flag*: **the hardest real term.** In sim it is free; on a real F140 it must be inferred from gripper-current/motor-torque sensing or a force/torque signal (the 2F-140 exposes current). Model as a noisy/delayed binary.
- *Both-grasps-active flags, exact attachment state*: privileged-only.

**Strategy question (state-based + DR now, camera later — CONFIRM the user's preference).** The evidence strongly supports keeping the RL loop camera-free first. VCD, FabricFlowNet, and FlingBot all trained on abstract/state or depth-only representations in SoftGym and transferred to real Franka/UR5 hardware (VCD zero-shot to 94–95% coverage on real cloth; FFN 0.857 mean IoU real folding). The dominant sim2real approach in current manipulation is **asymmetric actor–critic + teacher–student distillation**: train a privileged-state teacher, then distill into a camera/point-cloud student via DAgger (Pinto et al. 2018 asymmetric AC; the pattern is used across recent manipulation and locomotion work). Isaac Lab *can* do tiled rendering in-loop (the 2025 Isaac Lab tech report, arXiv 2511.04831, and docs recommend up to ~256–512 cameras on high-end GPUs), but with the project's cloth env already at a **64-env throughput knee (~400 env·steps/s)** on the A6000, adding RTX rendering in-loop would collapse throughput. **Confirm the user's instinct: keep cameras out of the RL loop; defer vision to a distillation stage with the door open.**

**Asymmetric actor–critic design.** Privileged critic gets: full particle field, true tautness, true coverage, attachment/grasp state, both-arm poses. Camera-realistic actor gets: down-sampled segmented point cloud, keypoints + visibility flags, coverage-from-mask, gripper-current-derived grasp flag, proprioception. **skrl-2.1.0 caveat**: whether skrl 2.1.0 truly separates policy vs critic observation groups for asymmetric AC is an open repo TODO — if it aliases them, switch the critic to RSL-RL (the designated fallback), which supports privileged critic observations natively.

**Load-bearing sim2real gaps, ordered to close:** (1) grasp-active flag estimation (free in sim, hard in reality) — build the current/force-based estimator early; (2) exact hem-corner position → keypoint-detector noise model; (3) tautness → move to critic-only or silhouette-estimate; (4) coverage is already near-real.

**Decision gate:** adopt in-loop tiled rendering only if a distilled point-cloud student loses > ~0.1 coverage vs the privileged teacher AND domain randomization + noise injection cannot close it — otherwise stay with distillation.

### Q4 — Existing sim-to-real work implementing a comparable pipeline; what transfers

**Survey (closest ancestors first):**
- **Doumanoglou et al. (2014, ICRA/ECCV)** — the closest ancestor: dual-arm, hanging-garment, keypoint-driven unfolding via active perception (random/Hough forests) in a POMDP. Engineered perception, no sim training. Transfers: the hang-then-grasp-second-point structure and semantic keypoints as targets.
- **Maitin-Shepard et al. (2010, ICRA)** — PR2 towel folding via multi-view geometric corner detection; in-air unfolding; success on all 50/50 unseen single-towel trials plus a pile of 5. Engineered, no sim. Transfers: depth/curvature corner cues, the reliability bar.
- **ICRA 2024 Cloth Competition (De Gusseme, Lips et al., arXiv 2508.16749)** — a standardized in-air unfolding grasp-selection benchmark (11 teams; 34 garments; 679 demonstrations); coverage normalized to a fully-unfolded in-air reference observation; final score is average coverage across the 16 evaluation trials per team. The winning AIR-jnu team used a hand-engineered geometric heuristic ("Intuitive Grasping Determination"): it fits a low-resolution polygon to the cloth's boundary and selects the *second-furthest vertex* from the held gripper as the grasp point, reaching ~0.60 average coverage; the ranking spread was 0.35–0.60. The paper reports the "surprisingly strong achievements of hand-engineered methods" over learning-based ones and a grasp-success vs coverage trade-off (top teams excelled via high coverage-given-success ≈ 0.7 rather than raw grasp-success rate), with a single-attempt grasp-selection framing. **This is the most directly comparable external benchmark and its coverage normalization (÷ fully-unfolded in-air reference) differs from the project's flat-area normalization — align before comparing.**
- **FlingBot (Ha & Song, 2021)** — dual-arm dynamic pick-stretch-fling; > 80% coverage in ≤ 3 actions in sim (SoftGym/FleX picker); real two-UR5 setup, *fine-tuned* on real hardware; documented that sim was highly sensitive to fling parameters while reality was robust — a cautionary sim2real note.
- **SpeedFolding (Avigal et al., 2022, IROS)** — bimanual smoothing+folding; BiMama-Net predicts *pairs* of gripper poses; reported verbatim: "After learning from 4300 human-annotated or self-supervised actions, the robot is able to fold garments from a random initial configuration in under 120s on average with a success rate of 93%," and "While prior work achieved 3–6 Folds Per Hour (FPH), SpeedFolding achieves 30 to 40 FPH." Learned from real actions, not sim RL. Transfers: single-network dual-pose prediction.
- **Cloth Funnels (Canberk et al., 2022, ICRA)** — canonicalized-alignment "cloth state funnel"; multi-arm, multi-primitive; DeepLabv3 keypoints. Transfers: the funnel-to-canonical idea (Q1 option d) and keypoint detection.
- **UniFolding / UFONet (Xue et al., 2023, CoRL)** — partial-point-cloud policy unifying unfold+fold; VR human demos + sim self-supervision + real human-in-the-loop; explicitly notes it *cannot* grasp a single layer reliably when multiple layers are present.
- **GarmentLab / DexGarmentLab (2024–2025, Isaac Sim)** — PBD+FEM garment benchmark on the same PhysX 5 stack; **directly relevant**: GarmentLab uses "attach blocks" for garment-gripper attachment and *DexGarmentLab explicitly states this fails to capture realistic interactions* (unnatural sagging; a single finger block touching the garment establishes attachment), motivating adhesion+friction+particle-scale parameters so dexterous hands grasp by force.
- **ClothesNet / Cloth3D** — the asset ecosystems (the project's t-shirt is a ClothesNet garment).
- **sewts VELUM (industrial, 2022–)** — the closest *deployed* analog: a robotic cell that picks crumpled laundered textiles from conveyors/containers and feeds folding machines, using two FANUC M-10iD arms and Ensenso S10 3D + uEye 2D cameras, CNNs + classical vision, FE material simulation (not RL); ~500–700 textiles/hr. Transfers: the conveyor-to-inspection industrial framing and multi-camera hem/corner detection; confirms commercial viability and that the hard part is perception + deformable handling, solved with engineered vision, not policy RL.

**Grasp modeling in sim vs hardware, and the sim2real mechanisms for PBD cloth.** Across SoftGym-based work (FFN, VCD, FlingBot) the sim grasp is an attach-picker (nearest particle bound to a sphere); on hardware it is emulated by a real pinch grasp, sometimes with modified fingertips (FlingBot's rubber/sponge tips). The reported PBD sim2real gap mechanisms: PhysX's iterative PBD solver produces unwanted stretch under gravity even at maximum stiffness (RGBench, 2025); fling-parameter sensitivity (FlingBot); and — dominant — real grasp failures (misgrasp, multi-layer) that attach-pickers do not model (DRAPER, 2024). Real2sim garment system-ID (parameter identification of bending/stretch stiffness, friction) is the mitigation direction.

**The gating question for Q5:** *Is there any published case of an attachment-based (kinematic) cloth grasp policy transferring to a physical gripper?* **Yes — but always with the picker emulated by a real pinch grasp, and never with the attachment modeling the grasp-failure physics.** VCD (zero-shot Franka, 94–95% real coverage), FabricFlowNet (0.857 real IoU), and FlingBot (real dual-UR5, after fine-tuning) all trained with SoftGym attach-pickers and ran on real parallel-jaw grippers. SoftGym's stated rationale is to "decouple the challenges in learning low-level grasping skills from high-level planning." **Crucially, no paper claims the attachment abstraction itself guarantees grasp transfer** — DRAPER/DeepCloth-ROB (2024) documents that real grasping is exactly where it breaks: "Misgrasp rates for the tweezers remain consistently below 10%, while the Franka Gripper shows higher rates between 16-22%," with a naive parallel gripper also grabbing multiple layers, versus a specialized tweezer end-effector exceeding 90% grasp success on edges/corners. So: an attachment-trained *planning/where-to-grasp* policy can transfer; the *grasp-success assumption* cannot, and must be handled by a grasp-success model or hardware/end-effector engineering.

### Q5 — Making the gripper physically more accurate within simulation constraints

**Evidence review on contact/friction pinch of PBD cloth.** PhysX 5 PBD particle cloth supports particle–rigid **adhesion and friction** parameters (adhesion, adhesion offset, particle-friction, dynamic_friction between particles and rigids), and DexGarmentLab demonstrates that with adhesion + friction + particle-scale tuning, dexterous hands can grasp and lift garments *by force* without attach blocks. So a two-sided friction pinch is *possible in principle* in PhysX 5. **But**: it is fragile — the PBD solver's gravity-stretch at finite iterations (RGBench), the ~5 mm particle spacing vs a thin 2-layer cloth, and the 60 Hz / 24-iteration budget make a stable multi-layer friction pinch hard to guarantee, and DexGarmentLab needed careful parameter tuning even for single-layer lifts. This is why virtually all sim benchmarks (SoftGym, GarmentLab default, FFN, VCD, FlingBot) use attach-style pickers: contact pinch is expensive, unstable, and unnecessary for learning *where/when* to grasp.

**Recommendation — fidelity ladder (ordered by cost, matching the study's occlusion-metric logic):**
- **(a) Keep attachment, gate success on physically-plausible pre-conditions (RECOMMENDED for training-eval bridge).** Require approach alignment, finger clearance, and single- vs multi-layer detection at the grasp point before allowing the anchor to form. Cheap, deterministic, and closes the largest reality gap (grasping the wrong layer).
- **(b) Stochastic grasp-failure/slip model calibrated from published statistics (RECOMMENDED for evaluation).** Draw grasp success from a distribution keyed to the grasp location (seam/hem vs mid-panel), estimated layer count, and pull force. Calibrate to DRAPER's Franka misgrasp (16–22%) and multi-layer rates, and to the layer-singulation literature (tactile-feedback 2-layer grasping is materially harder than 1-layer). This lets you *measure* whether the idealized training grasp changes policy ranking.
- **(c) Force-limited attachments.** Detach when the anchor constraint force exceeds a calibrated pinch capacity — natural given the two-attachment stretch already runs near tautness 1.15. Medium cost; a good intermediate for the stretch dynamics.
- **(d) Full contact pinch — validation study only, not training.** Use PhysX adhesion+friction for a one-off study to check that the attachment abstraction does not distort the coverage ranking.

**Real 2F-140 behavior and matching knobs.** Per Robotiq's official 2F-85/2F-140 instruction manual, the 2F-140 has a 0–140 mm stroke, an adjustable grip force from a minimum of 10 N to a maximum of 125 N, and a 2.5 kg maximum recommended payload; on cloth it tends to grab multiple layers with a naive top-down pinch (DRAPER). Sim knobs that matter for matching: mimic-joint drive stiffness/effort (already 11.25 / 10 N), the virtual-tip calibration offset (the known asset quirk where body frames co-locate at the flange), and — if moving to contact — particle friction/adhesion and solid rest offset.

**Final recommendation (training vs evaluation grasp + validation experiment):** **Train with the deterministic attachment (fast, stable, learns where/when to grasp); evaluate with the stochastic grasp-success gate (b) plus the pre-condition gate (a).** Validate with the same logic the study used for its occlusion metric: run the best checkpoints under both the idealized and the stochastic-gated grasp and confirm the *policy ranking is preserved* (i.e. the checkpoint that wins under attachment also wins under the failure model). If ranking flips, the gap is load-bearing and you promote the pre-condition gate into training. **Decision gate:** if the stochastic-gate evaluation drops success by more than a pre-registered threshold (e.g. > 0.15 absolute) and reorders checkpoints, move grasp-failure modeling into the training loop; otherwise keep it evaluation-only.

## 3. Roadmap (ordered, decision-gated)

**Stage 0 — Chaining seams & re-thresholding (thesis-critical).** Generate the real Task-1→2 terminal bank (including the 5–7% post-latch slips); raise Task-2 gate 0.50 → 0.65; add tautness-pulse reward. *Success:* Task 2 trains on real holder states with no throughput regression. *Effort:* ~3–5 agent-days + 2–3 GPU-days. *Dependency:* Task-1 snapshot hook (exists). *Abort/fallback:* if the real bank destabilizes Task 2, keep the synthetic hanging bank but inject the slip distribution.

**Stage 1 — Task-2 curriculum for the high hem corner (thesis-critical).** Bank-filter for low/reachable hem-corner starts, reach-assist resets, target-conditioned grasp curriculum. *Success:* hem↔hem grasp rate > 0.6 (from < 0.1). *Effort:* ~5–8 agent-days + 5–10 GPU-days (seed sweep on HPC). *Abort/fallback:* if grasp rate stays < 0.3, fall back to oracle lowest/accessible-corner target and bank 0.679–0.713.

**Stage 2 — Learned Task-1 grasp-point head (thesis-critical).** Add keypoint + border-distance observations and a downstream-coverage-shaped terminal bonus. *Success:* realized Task-2 coverage from Task-1 holds beats the 0.713 arbitrary-first-grasp oracle. *Effort:* ~5–8 agent-days + 5–10 GPU-days. *Abort:* revert to highest-point + oracle-target Task 2.

**Stage 3 — Cooperative Task 2: movable holder → single joint policy (thesis-critical → stretch).** Start with scripted-but-repositioning robot 1; then a single joint policy over both arms' actions. *Success:* coverage ≥ 0.65 (start) trending to 0.75 at grasp rate ≥ 0.9. *Effort:* ~8–12 agent-days + 10–20 GPU-days. *Abort:* ship single-agent oracle-target policy.

**Stage 4 — Asymmetric critic + observation contract (thesis-critical).** Split actor (camera-realistic) vs critic (privileged); verify skrl 2.1.0 separates observation groups, else switch to RSL-RL. *Success:* no coverage regression with the camera-realistic actor + calibrated noise. *Effort:* ~5–8 agent-days + 5–10 GPU-days.

**Stage 5 — Grasp-fidelity evaluation gate (thesis-critical, low cost).** Add the stochastic grasp-success + pre-condition gate to *evaluation*; run the ranking-preservation study. *Effort:* ~4–6 agent-days + 2–5 GPU-days.

**Stage 6 — MAPPO/IPPO ablation (stretch).** Only if Stage 3 shows a coordination failure. *Effort:* ~8–15 agent-days incl. DirectMARLEnv bridge + gymnasium pin. *Abort:* keep single joint policy.

**Stage 7 — Teacher–student vision distillation (stretch / future work).** DAgger a point-cloud student from the privileged teacher; in-loop tiled rendering only if distillation fails the gate.

**Stage 8 — Contact-physics pinch validation (stretch).** One-off PhysX adhesion+friction study.

## 4. Risk Register

- **Chaining distribution shift (#1).** Real terminal banks differ from synthetic banks; the 5–7% Task-1 slips propagate. *Mitigation:* generate banks forward, include slip episodes, gate each seam; keep synthetic-bank fallback with injected slip distribution.
- **MARL integration with manager-based envs.** DirectMARLEnv is direct-workflow only; skrl MARL breaks under gymnasium ≥ 1.0.0 (`env.state()`), maturity unverified in-repo. *Mitigation:* prefer the single joint policy (no MARL infra needed); if MAPPO is required, pin gymnasium 0.29.1 and budget a rewrite.
- **Grasp-fidelity rabbit hole.** Contact pinch of multi-layer PBD cloth is unstable at 60 Hz/24 iters. *Mitigation:* keep attachment for training; use stochastic gates for evaluation; contact physics for a bounded validation study only.
- **skrl asymmetric-AC uncertainty.** May alias actor/critic observation groups. *Mitigation:* verify early; RSL-RL fallback.
- **Throughput ceiling.** 64-env knee (~400 env·steps/s) caps sample budgets and forbids in-loop rendering. *Mitigation:* single-policy sample efficiency, HPC seed sweeps, camera-free RL loop.
- **Single-garment generalization.** One 11k-particle t-shirt; design breaks for other garments/resolutions. *Mitigation:* flag as out-of-scope; note in future work.

### Known uncertainties flagged
- All project coverage numbers use *this project's* metric (rasterized silhouette ÷ flat area) on one garment; the ICRA 2024 competition normalizes to a fully-unfolded in-air reference and FFN/VCD use IoU/coverage differently — align normalizations before any cross-paper comparison.
- The heuristics study used static anchors, not arms; arm kinematics, reachability, and arm-induced camera occlusion may shave the calibrated ceilings (the 2.9 cm holder-IK residual and the x-translation-free tensegrity base are concrete examples).
- skrl MAPPO/IPPO exist in the training script but have never been run in this repo, and the known gymnasium ≥ 1.0.0 / `env.state()` breakage means their maturity here is unverified — a strong additional reason to prefer the single joint policy.
- The PBD cloth is one 11k-particle t-shirt at one resolution; garment generalization is out of scope, but the observation contract (keypoints, coverage-from-mask) and the grasp-fidelity ladder are the components most likely to break first for other garments.
